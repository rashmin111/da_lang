A Whisper in the Wind
The next day, Tom couldn’t stop thinking about the clock. He kept the little box in his pocket and touched the feather from time to time. No one else seemed to notice anything strange. Grandma made pancakes, and Grandpa read the newspaper. But Tom was waiting for midnight.
That night, the clock ticked louder. Tick. Tick. Tick. Then it glowed again.
"Tom," it said softly. "It’s time."
Tom pressed the button. The screen blinked and showed a new message.
Riddle Two:
"I have keys but no locks.
I make music but I’m not a box.
What am I?"
Tom sat up straight. He whispered, “A piano?”
The clock made a cheerful ding! again. "Yes. You are learning quickly."
A new message appeared: “Go to the music room.”
Tom blinked. “Music room?”
Then he remembered. His grandparents once showed him a tiny back room full of old furniture—and a dusty piano.
He tiptoed through the house and opened the door to the small room. The piano stood quietly in the moonlight, covered in a thin cloth. He pulled the cloth away.
On the piano keys was a small envelope. He opened it. Inside was a photo of a young boy holding the same clock, many years ago.
On the back it said: “I played the game too. Good luck!”
Tom’s heart beat faster. Someone else had played this game before him. But who was he? His grandpa? Someone else?
He returned to his room and placed the photo next to the feather.
"This is the person who made me," said the clock. "He was kind and clever. He built me to help children find courage."
Tom couldn’t sleep for a long time. His adventure was only just beginning.

La Voz de Ayer
Tom no podía dejar de pensar en la foto.
Cuando volvió la medianoche, el reloj lo esperaba.
"¿Estás listo para el siguiente acertijo?"
“Sí.”
El texto apareció:
"No tengo boca, pero puedo hablar.
No tengo oídos, pero puedo oír.
No tengo cuerpo, pero vivo contigo.
¿Qué soy?"
Tom lo pensó. “¿No tiene boca pero habla? ¿Vive conmigo?”
Miró la foto nuevamente. Pensó en recuerdos, en palabras que viven incluso cuando las personas no están.
“¿Un recuerdo?” dijo.
"Correcto," dijo el reloj.
Un nuevo compartimiento se abrió. Dentro había una hoja de papel doblada. Al abrirla, encontró una carta escrita a mano, firmada por su abuelo, a quien nunca conoció.
"Querido quien lea esto,
Tal vez el reloj aún recuerde.
Guarda lo que importa.
Escucha cuando el silencio hable."
Tom tragó saliva. “¿Cómo puede un reloj recordar?”
El reloj respondió:
"Fui hecho no solo para contar el tiempo,
sino para recordar el amor."
