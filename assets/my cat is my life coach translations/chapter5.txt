The Great Escape 
 It was a quiet evening. Too quiet. I was reading a book and sipping tea. Ginger was... not in sight. That’s always a bad sign.
“Ginger?” I called.
No reply.
I checked her favorite places: the windowsill, the laundry basket, the fruit bowl (yes, she fits). Nothing. Then I noticed the window. It was open. My heart dropped. I ran to the window and looked out. No Ginger. Just the neighbour’s clothesline and a very angry pigeon.
“Ginger!” I shouted, already imagining posters: Lost cat. Answers to no name. May steal socks.
Then I heard a soft thump behind me. I turned. Ginger was on the table, licking butter off my toast.
I stared at her. “You were here the whole time?!”
She meowed like, “Obviously.”
“But the window!”
She yawned.
I looked back at the open window, then at her. “You faked your escape so you could eat in peace, didn’t you?”
She didn’t answer. She was too busy chewing.
That night, I closed the window, locked the butter away, and set a new rule: Never trust a cat with a plan. Ginger, of course, ignored the rule.
